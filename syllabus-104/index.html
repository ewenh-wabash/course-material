Transcript

Hello everybody! And welcome—this is MUS-104, Sound and Scary Movies. And I'm Hunter Ewen, your professor. I'm so excited you're here.

This is a brand new class, and I'm a brand new professor at Wabash—so we'll be figuring some of this out together, as we go.

Music classes are always a little bit weird, because it can be hard to tell what you've signing up for. Some music classes are like language classes, some are like history, some like philosophy, some language arts, some math, some are like PE.

This class is going to be like a book club. We're going to watch a lot...we're going to discuss a lot. You'll see some movies you know...and some you've never heard of. We'll talk about filmmaking and film scoring technique, creative practices, genre, culture, aesthetics.

By the end of this class, I want you to learn about the canon of scary movies and to recognize the classic soundtracks.

I want you to develop critical listening skills—to understand the creative decisions that go into making scary movies scary.

I want you to understand the transformative power that music has over film...how it can take a neutral scene and make it scary, or silly, or exciting, or boring. And I want you to understand the deep connection between film music and history, culture, aesthetic, and tradition.

I want you to better recognize 20th century styles, and how film genres relate to music, visual art, dance, prose. And I want you to understand how scary movies serve as a window into what our society is most worried about at any given time—the "truth" hiding behind fiction. Subtext, nuance, empathy, self-reflection, open-mindedness.

I want you to learn to listen deeply, to watch actively—and with purpose, and to become better, more informed citizens because of it. Your brain is a muscle, and flexing it...training it...in this class will help you develop in other parts of your life.

Music skills are life skills—and a foundation in music helps in the boardroom, helps in the courtroom, helps in the operating room, helps us live better, more reflective lives.

So I'm not interested in having you memorize what year William Peter Blatty wrote The Exorcist (1971). But I am going to demand that you engage with creative and challenging work. That you communicate authentically and effectively. That you take subjects where there isn't a right answer, like art, and make real insights from them.

This isn't a science class or a math class. There's not a clear pathway from question to answer. This is something much harder. In music, the questions often are the answers.

Music is a language of communication, of opinion, of emotion. And I'm here to help grow your communication skills. So feel free to reach out. Visit my office. Keep me in the loop. Email, email, email!

In fact, if you made it to the end of this video, congratulations for fully engaging with this content! Send me an email right now, and I'll give you some bonus points!

Thank you for joining. I'm excited to get to know you better. Bye!

I left CU in 2016 to move to Columbus, Ohio and work in the music technology industry. My speciaty was building tools for empowering creativity, leading teams of music technologists at Amper Music, Shutterstock/PremiumBeat, and eventually BandLab. In a decade of service, I'm proud to have never trained using anyone else's music, never scraped a user's data, and never strayed from the ethical frameworks we established. At the time, I was still moonlighting as an educator (private lessons and teaching music/computer programming at IvyTech), but my days were spent leading teams and coding project.

In 2026, my wife and I packed up and moved to Crawfordsville, so I could get back to the thing I enjoyed most about my career—teaching! Nowadays, I love puzzles, making wine, food, bicycling, and other esoteric interests.

    on fear...

    This is not a scary class, but we will watch, hear, and discuss scary things here. It’s okay to be frightened—the point of the media we’re studying is to scare you. But if something is too much, you don’t have to force yourself to endure it. In this class, It is always okay to leave if a film, score, or conversation becomes too much. It’s okay to close your eyes, plug your ears, or walk out of the room if something feels too intense or if you need a breather. It’s okay to look up jumpscares or trigger warningsbeforehand and identify movies you don’t want to consume for class. If there are specific movies or topics you want to avoid, send me an email so we can figure out another way for you to engage with the material.

    Some good resources for content warnings:

    • DoesTheDogDie.com (trigger and content warnings)
    • IMDB.com's parental guides (sex/nudity, violence/gore, alcohol/drugs, frightening scenes)
    • WheresTheJump.com (timestampted jumpscare locations)
    • Jumpscare Subtitles (my script that takes jumpscare or translation subtitles and syncs them to the movie)

    Our goal is to study fiction, but sometimes reality sneaks in. There are lots of movies we won't watch because real-life bad things found their way into the fiction. This course will focus on fictional horror by avoiding the following:

    • films with actual, real-life physical violence 🔎
    • films that make light of real-world atrocities 🔎
    • films with actual, real-life sexual abuse or nonconsensual sexual activity 🔎
    • films where consent cannot be given 🔎
    • films with actual, real-life animal abuse 🔎

    While these subjects or films may come up in conversation, we will not be viewing films like these for this class. I encourage you to use these criteria as you are identifying projects for independent study and presentation.

    N.B. The film industry has a history of offscreen abuse, much of which is hidden. We must acknowledge and reflect on this abuse. In class, we will explore The Shining, The Exorcist, and The Birds, which are all films where the actors suffered offscreen abuse.

    on being wrong...

    I WANT YOU TO BE WRONG. I want you to ask why. I want you to be unsure. I want you to be confused. I want you to change your mind.

    Saying “I don’t know” builds radical trust. It strips away ego and creates room for curiosity. It signals confidence without pretending to have all the answers. Some of the most powerful intellectual moves we can make are also the simplest: I don’t know. I might be wrong. Tell me more. I hadn’t thought about it that way. Keep in mind: this is not med school or law school class. The work we’re doing here is deeply personal, creative, and uninterpretable. You are not here to memorize the most facts or produce the most “right” answers.

    The most successful student in my class is the one willing to reach down deep in their soul—to grab hold of the strange, difficult, beautiful thing that makes us human, and be brave enough to wrench it back up and examine it in the light of day. My goal is to help you understand the world better. To help you understand yourselves better. To help you understand each other better.

    So get something wrong. Be curious. Change your mind. Take intellectual risks.

    The red tape

    Course policies

    In general, I am flexible and open to rescheduling, make-up work, excused absences, quiz retakes, etc. However, you and I will need to agree on any change beforehand (and document in writing) of the scope and timing of the work. In general, if anything comes up that will affect your semester, email or visit me as soon as possible so we can help build workarounds.

    The only truly inflexible rule I have is that I am unable to accept work beyond the end of the semester.

    Attendance

    I expect you to attend every class and event. However, with enough notice, I am generous with excused absences. Let me know as soon as you know you'll need to miss class—for sports, illness, emergencies, etc.

    If you miss a class (excused or not), I still expect you to engage with the day's material to receive full attendance/participation points. It is not possible for me to re-teach a previous lecture following an absense, so asking "what did I miss in class yesterday" is not a viable way to engage with that day's material. Contact me so we can agree on the scope of make-up work required to receive full points.

    Late Work

    This class moves quickly, and it's important work is completed on time. That being said, I understand that other factors sometimes cause delays in turning in work. My blanket policy is to take 30% off for all late work unless an extension is granted in advance.

    Extra Credit

    I love extra credit! Reach out or drop by my office if you have an idea or if you want me to come up with some suggestions. My only policy around extra credit is to talk to me first, so we can agree on a timeframe and points value. Each bonus point works out to about 2% of your final grade, so for small projects or rewards, I might give fractions of a bonus point too!

    AI Policy

    tl;dr don't use AI

    Using AI for this class is like going to the gym, sitting on a bench, and watching a video of someone else working out.

    Your brain is a muscle. Your attention span is a muscle. Your analytic prowess is a muscle. You are here to exercise your brain. Generatie AI use can lead to cognitive decline, and it can make you more dependent on it in the future (cognitive intertia). Using it in class is lazy and rude—and it's an academic integrity violation. Last year, we had a record breaking amount of academic dishonestly, and that was overwhelmingly from unauthorized use of AI. It’s too easy to circumvent the friction that you’re here to experience.

    I don’t want polished, lazy, ChatGPT-fueled responses from you. I want weird, buzzy, human writing that crackles with the uncertainty of original thought. Do not use generative AI, including but not limited to:

    • generate part of an assignment
    • get summaries of movies
    • wordsmith your written work
    • summarize ideas or arguments
    • help format an email or other written document

    Use of generative AI in any similar context is a strike on your academic record—and possible expulsion if you already have a strike.

    If you feel yourself slipping, or you’re running out of time on an assignment, or you’re stressed about other commitments, and you start to reach for ChatGPT, REACH OUT TO ME. I want you to be successful in this class, and I will bend over backwards to make sure you can find success without AI.

    Academic integrity

    The Gentleman’s Rule applies to this course in several ways but particularly concerning academic integrity.

    Unless stated in the assignment guidelines, collaboration on assignments is not allowed. More generally, plagiarism (quoting, paraphrasing, or taking ideas from any source— machine or human—without explicit citation), duplicate submission of the same work, or the unauthorized possession of tests and other assessment materials is strictly forbidden. Academic dishonesty may result in the failing of the assignment and further disciplinary action by the College. Visit the Academic Honesty Guide for more:

    Negligence or ignorance are not acceptable excuses for violation of this policy. It is possible to commit plagiarism without fully knowing it so, when in doubt, ask me for guidance.

    Classroom Code of Conduct

    "Beware of artists. They mix with all classes of society and are therefore most dangerous."
    - Queen Victoria.


    How it Works

    Music is the language of emotions, and the deeper you get into studying music the more you realize how deeply interconnected music is with other social and political structures. Even though we're studying music...other things may come up. The term "safe space" has become politically charged, so I won't use that here. But I do want to create an environment where we feel comfortable, supported, and free around one another. I'm open to updating this code of conduct based on your feedback, but here's a list of a few tenets, based on the gentleman's rule I'd like to see and model in my classroom:

    • We've got your back
    • We don't yuck each other's yums
    • We don't punish others for their opinions
    • We have a duty to be wrong in front of each other
    • We listen and try not to interrupt
    • We respect personal choices and boundaries (especially when it comes to scary stuff)



    What it Means

    • We don't dunk on other people
    • You don't have to hide your real thoughts in this class because you're worried they'll conflict with my or your classmates opinions
    • You can feel comfortable "trying on" a new point of view, playing devil's advocate for a bit, or being confidently wrong
    • There's no such thing as "guilty pleasure" music...only "pleasure" music
    • Arguments aren't meant to be "won" in here
    • There are no penalties for having opinions
