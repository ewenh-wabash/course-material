## *on fear...*

**This is not a scary class**, but we will watch, hear, and discuss scary things here. It’s okay to be frightened—the point of the media we’re studying is to scare you. But if something is too much, you don’t have to force yourself to endure it. In this class, It is always okay to leave if a film, score, or conversation becomes too much. It’s okay to close your eyes, plug your ears, or walk out of the room if something feels too intense or if you need a breather. It’s okay to look up [jumpscares](https://wheresthejump.com) or [trigger warnings](https://www.doesthedogdie.com) beforehand and identify movies you don’t want to consume for class. If there are specific movies or topics you want to avoid, send me an [email](mailto:ewenh@wabash.edu) so we can figure out another way for you to engage with the material.

Some good resources for content warnings:

- [DoesTheDogDie.com](https://www.doesthedogdie.com) (trigger and content warnings)
- [IMDB.com](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0075005/parentalguide)'s parental guides (sex/nudity, violence/gore, alcohol/drugs, frightening scenes)
- [WheresTheJump.com](https://wheresthejump.com) (timestampted jumpscare locations)
- [Jumpscare Subtitles](https://ewenh-wabash.github.io/course-material/subtitles.html) (my script that takes [jumpscare](https://wheresthejump.com) or [translation](https://kickasssubtitles.com/en/search) subtitles and syncs them to the movie - download the jumpscare files for the [courselist](https://ewenh-wabash.github.io/course-material/subtitle_files.zip))

Our goal is to study fiction, but sometimes reality sneaks in. There are lots of movies we won't watch because real-life bad things found their way into the fiction. This course will focus on **fictional** horror by avoiding the following:

- films with actual, real-life physical violence [🔎](imdb.com/title/tt1522846/parentalguide)
- films that make light of real-world atrocities [🔎](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt1935896/parentalguide)
- films with actual, real-life sexual abuse or nonconsensual sexual activity [🔎](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0103772/parentalguide)
- films where consent cannot be given [🔎](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0218871/parentalguide)
- films with actual, real-life animal abuse [🔎](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0078935/parentalguide)

While these subjects or films may come up in conversation, we will not be viewing films like these for this class. I encourage you to use these criteria as you are identifying projects for independent study and presentation.

N.B. The film industry has a history of offscreen [abuse](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0063269279), much of which is hidden. We must acknowledge and reflect on this abuse. In class, we will explore [The Shining](https://chscommunicator.com/90269/opinion/2024/04/the-working-environment-of-the-shining/), [The Exorcist](https://vocal.media/horror/the-real-and-terrifying-injury-caused-on-the-set-of-the-exorcist), and [The Birds](https://collider.com/the-birds-production-history-explained/), which are all films where the actors suffered offscreen abuse.

## *on being wrong...*

**I WANT YOU TO BE WRONG**. I want you to ask why. I want you to be unsure. I want you to be confused. I want you to change your mind.

Saying “I don’t know” builds radical trust. It strips away ego and creates room for curiosity. It signals confidence without pretending to have all the answers. Some of the most powerful intellectual moves we can make are also the simplest: **I don’t know. I might be wrong. Tell me more. I hadn’t thought about it that way.**
Keep in mind: this is not med school or law school class. The work we’re doing here is deeply personal, creative, and uninterpretable. You are not here to memorize the most facts or produce the most “right” answers.

The most successful student in my class is the one willing to reach down deep in their soul—to grab hold of the strange, difficult, beautiful thing that makes us human, and be brave enough to wrench it back up and examine it in the light of day.
My goal is to help you understand the world better. To help you understand yourselves better. To help you understand each other better.

So get something wrong. Be curious. Change your mind. Take intellectual risks.

### AI Policy

**tl;dr don't use AI**

Using AI for this class is like going to the gym, sitting on a bench, and watching a video of someone else working out.

Your brain is a muscle. Your attention span is a muscle. Your analytic prowess is a muscle. You are here to exercise your brain. Generative AI use can lead to [cognitive decline](ai-cognitive-decline.pdf), and it can [make you more dependent](ai-dependence-and-cognitive-intertia.pdf) on it in the future (cognitive intertia). Using it in class is lazy and rude—and it's an academic integrity violation. Last year, we had a record breaking amount of academic dishonestly, and that was overwhelmingly from unauthorized use of AI. It’s too easy to circumvent the friction that you’re here to experience.

I don’t want polished, lazy, ChatGPT-fueled responses from you. I want weird, buzzy, human writing that crackles with the uncertainty of original thought. Do not use generative AI, including but not limited to:

- generating part of an assignment
- getting summaries of movies
- wordsmithing your written work
- summarizing ideas or arguments
- helping format an email or other written document

**Use of generative AI in any similar context is a strike on your academic record**—and possible expulsion if you already have a strike.

If you feel yourself slipping, or you’re running out of time on an assignment, or you’re stressed about other commitments, and you start to reach for ChatGPT, [**REACH OUT TO ME**](mailto:ewenh@wabash.edu). I want you to be successful in this class, and I will bend over backwards to make sure you can find success without AI.

### Emergency Response

In case of a fire, proceed from the classroom, out the nearest exit, and move at least 50 yards away from the building. You should join your instructor and class outside to make sure that everyone got out of the building ok. Then move as a group to the Chapel to report to the emergency personnel in charge.

In the event of a severe weather storm, proceed either to the basement and shelter in the basement hallway near the green rooms or in the shelter location behind Salter Hall.

### Supplemental Instruction (SI)

Generally, music courses are not covered under SI Sessions, but this may useful for other classes. SI Sessions are highly structured, peer-led group study designed to give students additional ways of learning the course material and processing new knowledge. SI is held twice a week, and each session will be distinct in content. Outside of class, the SI Leader and instructor will confer on the material and concepts that will be most useful to study. Attendance at SI, while encouraged, is strictly voluntary. SI Sessions are not the place to go over homework questions; you can use the QSC and office hours for that. SI Sessions are, however, a place to gain fuller mastery of the material and to collaborate with peers who are committed to on-task group study. Based on data we collected from SI attendance at Wabash in previous semesters, we can predict higher final course grades for students who attend at least one-quarter of the SI Sessions. For more information about SI at Wabash and the session schedule for all courses, visit [wabash.edu/ace/si](https://www.wabash.edu/ace/si).

#### How it Works

Music is the language of emotions, and the deeper you get into studying music the more you realize how deeply interconnected music is with other social and political structures. Even though we're studying music...other things may come up. The term "safe space" has become politically charged, so I won't use that here. But I do want to create an environment where we feel comfortable, supported, and free around one another. I'm open to updating this code of conduct based on your feedback, but here's a list of a few tenets, based on the [gentleman's rule](https://www.wabash.edu/aboutwabash/rule) I'd like to see and model in my classroom:

- We've got your back
- We don't yuck each other's yums
- We don't punish others for their opinions
- We have a duty to be wrong in front of each other
- We listen and try not to interrupt
- We respect personal choices and boundaries (especially when it comes to scary stuff)

---

#### What it Means

- We don't dunk on other people
- You don't have to hide your real thoughts in this class because you're worried they'll conflict with my or your classmates opinions
- You can feel comfortable "trying on" a new point of view, playing devil's advocate for a bit, or being confidently wrong
- There's no such thing as "guilty pleasure" music...only "pleasure" music
- Arguments aren't meant to be "won" in here
- There are no penalties for having opinions
