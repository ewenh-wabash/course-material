## Grades

Much of this class is creative, and I want to foster an environment where you feel comfortable bringing that creativity without worrying too much about grades. To that end, my goals this semester are that you:

- gain a minimum level of skill, understanding, and mastery of music reading/writing/making
- work hard improve your existing skillset (some of you will come from very different backgrounds and have very different amounts of prior music training)

And to accomplish those goals, I’m using a system of evaluation called ***ungrading***. Here’s what ungrading means:

- Don’t expect letter or number grades on your daily work
- You’ll get regular feedback from me on assignments, so you’ll know where you’re nailing it and where you need work
- You’ll be asked to fill out a weekly self-evaluation of your work, your strengths, your challenges, and how I can help. I will respond to this survey with additional feedback and guidance

Final grades, which are required by the college and contribute to your GPA, are determind (by me) as a summation of projects and assessments and any extra credit. There is no D+ or D-. See more information on credit/no credit and GPA on the [Academic Policies](https://bulletin.wabash.edu/academic-policies/grading-system/) page.

You are welcome to resubmit all or part of any work you feel could be improved (up until the final exam). And you may ask me to provide an estimate of your overall grade at any point in the semester.

## *on being wrong...*

**I WANT YOU TO BE WRONG**. I want you to ask why. I want you to be unsure. I want you to be confused. I want you to change your mind.

Saying “I don’t know” builds radical trust. It strips away ego and creates room for curiosity. It signals confidence without pretending to have all the answers. Some of the most powerful intellectual moves we can make are also the simplest: **I don’t know. I might be wrong. Tell me more. I hadn’t thought about it that way.**
Keep in mind: this is not med school or law school class. The work we’re doing here is deeply personal, creative, and uninterpretable. You are not here to memorize the most facts or produce the most “right” answers.

The most successful student in my class is the one willing to reach down deep in their soul—to grab hold of the strange, difficult, beautiful thing that makes us human, and be brave enough to wrench it back up and examine it in the light of day.
My goal is to help you understand the world better. To help you understand yourselves better. To help you understand each other better.

So get something wrong. Be curious. Change your mind. Take intellectual risks.

### Self Evaluation

I usually find that students are pretty good at self-evaluation (and are often more self-harsh graders than I will be). But there is something called the [Dunning-Kruger effect](https://www.openmindmag.org/articles/david-dunning-on-expertise) where people with limited experience in a certain area tend to overestimate their knowledge and achievement in that area. Because of this, I will be making the final determination about your grades, but since we’ll be in regular communication throughout the semester, it is a collaboration and there should be no surprises!

I will have minimum competency expectations for things like vocabulary, ear training, and written-music literacy—to try to make sure you’re prepared for your next music class. But in general, I’m way more interested in how you work to improve your skills from the beginning of the semester to the end. You are here to exercise your brain, to develop new skills, to build on your existing skillset, and to grow as a person and a musician.

### AI Policy

**tl;dr don't use AI**

Using AI for this class is like going to the gym, sitting on a bench, and watching a video of someone else working out.

Your brain is a muscle. Your attention span is a muscle. Your analytic prowess is a muscle. You are here to exercise your brain. Generative AI use can lead to [cognitive decline](ai-cognitive-decline.pdf), and it can [make you more dependent](ai-dependence-and-cognitive-intertia.pdf) on it in the future (cognitive intertia). Using it in class is lazy and rude—and it's an academic integrity violation. Last year, we had a record breaking amount of academic dishonestly, and that was overwhelmingly from unauthorized use of AI. It’s too easy to circumvent the friction that you’re here to experience.

I don’t want polished, lazy, ChatGPT-fueled responses from you. I want weird, buzzy, human writing that crackles with the uncertainty of original thought. Do not use generative AI, including but not limited to:

- generating part of an assignment
- getting summaries of movies
- wordsmithing your written work
- summarizing ideas or arguments
- helping format an email or other written document

**Use of generative AI in any similar context is a strike on your academic record**—and possible expulsion if you already have a strike.

If you feel yourself slipping, or you’re running out of time on an assignment, or you’re stressed about other commitments, and you start to reach for ChatGPT, [**REACH OUT TO ME**](mailto:ewenh@wabash.edu). I want you to be successful in this class, and I will bend over backwards to make sure you can find success without AI.

#### How it Works

Music is the language of emotions, and the deeper you get into studying music the more you realize how deeply interconnected music is with other social and political structures. Even though we're studying music...other things may come up. The term "safe space" has become politically charged, so I won't use that here. But I do want to create an environment where we feel comfortable, supported, and free around one another. I'm open to updating this code of conduct based on your feedback, but here's a list of a few tenets, based on the [gentleman's rule](https://www.wabash.edu/aboutwabash/rule) I'd like to see and model in my classroom:

- We've got your back
- We don't yuck each other's yums
- We don't punish others for their opinions
- We have a duty to be wrong in front of each other
- We listen and try not to interrupt
- We respect personal choices and boundaries

---

#### What it Means

- We don't dunk on other people
- You don't have to hide your real thoughts in this class because you're worried they'll conflict with my or your classmates opinions
- You can feel comfortable "trying on" a new point of view, playing devil's advocate for a bit, or being confidently wrong
- There's no such thing as "guilty pleasure" music...only "pleasure" music
- Arguments aren't meant to be "won" in here
- There are no penalties for having opinions
